1. Strategy Mechanics (How the Strategy Works)

The strategy is a systematic, intraday mean-reversion and expansion model that exploits institutional liquidity pools on a 1-minute chart. The core logic is built around two key phases: Liquidity Cleansing and Displacement Confirmation.

Key Execution Rules:

  • Liquidity Pool Identification: The algorithm maps active support and resistance levels (daily/session highs and lows) dynamically using ATR-based proximity parameters to prevent level crowding.
  • The Sweep: An entry sequence is initiated when price briefly wicks past a level (cleansing stop-losses) but fails to close beyond it.
  • Confirmation (Displacement): The strategy requires a rapid reversal (displacement) in the opposite direction, verified by the rejection of a Fair Value Gap (FVG) or a Break of Structure (BOS).
  • Risk Management & Risk Cap: The stop-loss is placed at the sweep extreme (defining R in points). To protect the account during erratic macro panic, a Risk Cap is applied at 60 points. Setups with R exceeding the cap are discarded.
  • Exit Structure: Trade execution utilizes a split exit mode (50% closed at 2.0R, remaining 50% trailed to breakeven targeting a secondary structural pivot).

2. Backtesting Methodology (How the Strategy Was Tested)

To ensure strategy robustness across multiple market regimes, we backtested the model continuously over an 11-year cycle (2015–2025) using a rolling walk-forward validation framework.

[  Train: 6 Months (1800 setups)  ] -> [ Test: 1 Week (Step=5 setups) ]
      [  Train: 6 Months (1800 setups)  ] -> [ Test: 1 Week ]
            [  Train: 6 Months (1800 setups)  ] -> [ Test: 1 Week ]

Technical Design:

  • Rolling Window: We utilized a 6-Month Rolling Window (1,800 setups). The model trains on the last 1,800 trade setups and predicts the next 5 setups out-of-sample (OOS). This prevents both memory lag (bias) and overfitting to noise (variance).
  • Machine Learning Engine: A LightGBM Classifier was retrained at each step using 11 structural features (e.g., sweep size, liquidity strength, session hours, FVG rejection).
  • Probability Calibration: The baseline success rate (win label) of setups is 14.1%. Because the LightGBM models are trained on 1,800 setups, their output probabilities are highly regularized and calibrated around this base rate. A probability threshold of 0.25 represents the top 90th percentile of setups and was used to trigger trades.
  • Session Constraint: Trades were strictly executed during the high-liquidity New York session (9:30 AM – 4:00 PM EST).

4. Margin Efficiency and Leveraged Futures (Capital Efficiency)

Intraday futures contracts provide unparalleled capital efficiency, allowing a portfolio to generate returns without tying up large pools of liquid cash.

  • Pledging Collateral (Double Yield): Futures margin requirements can be met by pledging stable, interest-bearing assets like U.S. Treasury Bills (T-Bills) or Money Market Funds. This allows you to earn the risk-free rate (~5% annualized) on 100% of your capital, while overlaying this intraday strategy on top.
  • Risk vs. Leverage: Leverage is not dangerous; improper position sizing is. The broker's margin requirement for 1 MNQ contract is only ~$1,800, but the contract controls $40,000 of Nasdaq value (22x leverage).
  • Safe Sizing: By sizing your contracts strictly to your target dollar stop-loss (e.g. risking exactly 2% of equity per trade), you ensure that your actual leverage remains highly conservative, preventing any margin liquidation risk while maximizing capital efficiency.

5. Portable Alpha & Portfolio Construction (Uncorrelated Returns)

The most valuable asset in institutional finance is uncorrelated alpha. A strategy with a lower Sharpe ratio (e.g., 0.41 or 0.66) is highly valuable if its correlation ($\rho$) to the stock market is near 0.0.

The Portfolio Diversification Math:

Assume you hold a traditional long equity market portfolio and overlay this uncorrelated MNQ strategy (ρ ≈ 0.0): * Core Equity Portfolio: Sharpe = 1.0 * MNQ Strategy: Sharpe = 0.66 (during trading periods)

If you allocate capital equally (50/50 risk weight) to both, the portfolio Sharpe ratio is: $$\text{Sharpe}_{\text{portfolio}} = \frac{0.5(1.0) + 0.5(0.66)}{\sqrt{0.5^2 + 0.5^2}} = \frac{0.83}{0.707} = \mathbf{1.17}$$

The combined portfolio Sharpe ratio (1.17) is significantly higher than both assets in isolation.

Furthermore, during the 2022 bear market (equity market down significantly), the MNQ strategy remained profitable (+8.0%), acting as a natural hedge. Adding this strategy smooths out the equity curve, lowers drawdowns, and allows the overall portfolio to generate returns during market downturns.
